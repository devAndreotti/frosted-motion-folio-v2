Electronic waste is not just about discarding technology. It involves natural resources, information security, public health, and sustainability.

Recently, I completed a University Extension activity in the Computer Science program at Universidade Paulista (UNIP), where I developed an eBook on the topic:

Electronic Waste // System Failure :: From Mineral Extraction to Hardware Circular Economy

The writing is aligned with two United Nations Sustainable Development Goals:

• SDG 12 — Responsible Consumption and Production
• SDG 13 — Climate Action

According to the Global E-waste Monitor, the world generated 62 million metric tons of electronic waste in a single year, while less than a quarter of that volume was formally recycled.

Smartphones, computers, and other devices concentrate precious materials such as gold, copper, lithium, and palladium. At the same time, improper disposal can release hazardous substances harmful to both the environment and public health.

Based on this topic, we produced this 22-page technical document covering:

• Global and Brazilian electronic waste overview;
• Hardware anatomy: printed circuit boards, batteries, displays, and modules;
• Environmental impacts and hazards of improper disposal;
• National Solid Waste Policy (PNRS — Brazilian Federal Law No. 12,305/2010);
• Data sanitization and secure wiping prior to device decommissioning;
• Reverse logistics, right-to-repair, and circular economy.
